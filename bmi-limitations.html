Limitations of BMI

BMI is a quick screening tool, and a blunt one. Here are the situations in which it misleads and what to use alongside it.

It cannot tell fat from muscle

Because it only uses weight and height, a muscular athlete and a sedentary person of the same size get the same score.

It ignores where fat is stored

Fat around the waist and organs raises risk more than fat on the hips and thighs. Two people with the same BMI can have different metabolic risk.

Age, sex and ethnicity

Older adults lose muscle, women carry more body fat than men at the same BMI, and risk begins at a lower BMI for many people of Asian descent. Single cut-offs cannot reflect all of this.

Groups for whom it is not suitable

Children and teens (see BMI for children), pregnant women, and people with significant muscle loss or oedema.

Better together

Pair BMI with waist circumference, waist-to-height ratio (keeping waist under half your height is a common target), blood tests and a conversation with your doctor. For the context, see BMI categories and the medical disclaimer.

Frequently asked questions

Why do doctors still use BMI?

It is free, fast and correlates with risk across large populations, which makes it a practical first screen.
