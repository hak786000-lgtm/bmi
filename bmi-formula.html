The BMI Formula

Body mass index is a simple ratio of mass to height squared. This page covers the equations, how to rearrange them, and where the index came from.

The equations

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

Rearranging for a target weight

To find the weight that would give a particular BMI, multiply the BMI by height squared. For a person 1.75 m tall, the healthy range is 18.5 × 3.0625 = 56.7 kg up to 24.9 × 3.0625 = 76.3 kg. This is how the healthy weight range is calculated.

Where it comes from

The formula was developed in the 1830s by the Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet, who was studying the average man. In 1972 physiologist Ancel Keys examined several formulas and found it was the best simple predictor of body fat across groups, and named it body mass index.

Why height is squared

Weight scales roughly with volume, but people do not grow equally in every direction. Squaring height is a practical compromise that works reasonably for average builds, though it can overestimate fatness in tall people and underestimate it in short people.

Frequently asked questions

Who invented BMI?

Adolphe Quetelet devised the ratio in the 19th century. The name body mass index came from Ancel Keys in 1972.
