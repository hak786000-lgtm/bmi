How BMI works

Body mass index compares your weight with your height. It divides weight in kilograms by height in metres squared. Two people of different heights can weigh the same and still land in different categories, because the formula scales weight to body size. The calculation is covered step by step in how to calculate BMI, and the underlying maths in the BMI formula guide.

BMI does not measure body fat directly. It is a quick, free way to flag whether a weight might deserve a closer look, which is why doctors use it as a first screen rather than a final verdict.

Why BMI matters

Large population studies link very low and very high BMI with higher rates of conditions such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease. Knowing where you sit gives you a reason to look at habits like activity, sleep and diet, and to ask your clinician about waist measurement, blood pressure and blood tests, which tell a much fuller story. Be aware of the limitations of BMI before drawing conclusions.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI for adults?

A BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 falls in the healthy weight range for most adults. It is a screening number, not a diagnosis.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so a muscular person can have a high BMI without excess body fat.

Can I use this calculator for my child?

No. Children's BMI is interpreted using age- and sex-specific percentiles, not the adult cut-offs.

Does this site store my measurements?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
