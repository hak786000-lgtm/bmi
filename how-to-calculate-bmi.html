How to Calculate BMI

You can work out your BMI with a basic calculator in under a minute. Here is the process, with examples in both measurement systems.

Method 1: metric (kg and cm)

  1. Convert your height to metres by dividing centimetres by 100.
  2. Multiply that number by itself.
  3. Divide your weight in kilograms by the result.

Example: 165 cm and 60 kg. Height is 1.65 m, squared is 2.7225, and 60 ÷ 2.7225 = 22.0.

Method 2: imperial (lb and inches)

  1. Convert your height to inches (feet × 12 plus the extra inches).
  2. Square it.
  3. Divide your weight in pounds by that number, then multiply by 703.

Example: 5 ft 9 in and 180 lb. Height is 69 in, squared is 4,761, and 180 ÷ 4,761 × 703 = 26.6.

Common mistakes

  • Using centimetres in the formula without converting to metres
  • Forgetting to square the height
  • Leaving out 703 when using pounds and inches
  • Rounding too early; keep decimals until the end

Check your answer

Compare the result with the BMI chart or let the calculator do it. For the algebra behind these steps, see the BMI formula page.

Frequently asked questions

Why is 703 used for imperial units?

It is a conversion factor that makes the imperial result match the metric result.
