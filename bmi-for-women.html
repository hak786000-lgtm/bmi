BMI for Women

Adult BMI cut-offs are identical for women and men, but women’s bodies change across life stages in ways the number cannot show.

Same categories, different composition

At the same BMI, women have a higher percentage of body fat than men on average. That is normal biology and part of why BMI is a screening tool, not a body-fat measurement.

Pregnancy

Standard BMI categories do not apply during pregnancy. Weight gain targets are set by clinicians using your pre-pregnancy BMI, so ask your midwife or doctor rather than using the calculator.

Menopause and ageing

Hormonal changes shift fat toward the abdomen and reduce muscle mass, so waist size and strength can change even when BMI stays the same. A waist of 80 cm (31.5 in) or more is commonly flagged for increased risk in women, and 88 cm (34.5 in) or more for high risk.

Healthy habits matter more than a number

Strength training protects bone and muscle, and a varied diet supports energy and iron levels. If you are concerned about disordered eating, speak to a health professional instead of using BMI as a goal.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use BMI while breastfeeding?

You can, but weight can fluctuate. Many clinicians suggest waiting until feeding patterns settle before drawing conclusions.
