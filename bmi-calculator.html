BMI Calculator

Type in your height and weight, choose your units and press Calculate. Results appear instantly with your category and healthy weight range.

How to use the calculator

Pick metric or imperial first. In metric, enter height in centimetres and weight in kilograms. In imperial, enter feet and inches for height and pounds for weight. Measure height without shoes and weigh yourself in light clothing, ideally at the same time of day.

Understanding your result

The number is placed on a colour scale from underweight to obesity, and the calculator also shows the weight span that would give a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 at your height.

A worked example

A person who is 170 cm tall and weighs 68 kg has a height of 1.70 m. Squared, that is 2.89. Dividing 68 by 2.89 gives a BMI of 23.5, which is in the healthy weight range. The same person at 85 kg would have a BMI of 29.4, which is overweight.

What to do next

If your BMI is outside the healthy range, treat it as a prompt rather than a diagnosis. Compare it on the BMI chart, read what each band means in BMI categories, and review the limits of BMI. A doctor can assess waist circumference, blood pressure and other measures that matter more for individual risk.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI for adults?

A BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 falls in the healthy weight range for most adults. It is a screening number, not a diagnosis.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Muscle weighs more than fat, so a muscular person can have a high BMI without excess body fat.

Why does the calculator reject some values?

Heights and weights outside realistic human limits are usually typing mistakes, such as entering inches in the centimetre box, so the tool asks you to check them.
